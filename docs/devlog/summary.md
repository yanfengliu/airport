# Devlog Summary

- 2026-11-26: **Our own models, made in Polyfork's manner** — for what Polyfork has no model of, the owner asked for models of our own made the way Polyfork's are and credited as ours. `tools/models/original/` holds a small kit for them (flat-coloured low-poly solids, named colour slots the app can change per instance, named parts that turn or slide about their pivots, poses as the same per-part matrices characters use) and the first five, written beside Polyfork's by the converter through one shared writer (`tools/models/format.ts`; Polyfork's files came out byte for byte the same): a double-deck airliner for the A380, built from the shape the stands judge it by, with its gear folding up in a 'gear-up' pose; and BART and Caltrain cars, plain and with a driving cab, at the simulation's car sizes, their doors sliding open in a six-frame 'doors' animation. `public/models/index.json` now gives every model its source and category. The A380 is drawn with ours; the trains are not drawn with theirs yet.
- 2026-11-26: **Aircraft on Polyfork's airliners** — every type but the A380 is now drawn with Polyfork's narrow-body or wide-body airliner fitted to its published length, span and height (a 757 is the narrow-body stretched a quarter longer, an E175 shrunk a fifth), with the airline's colour on its tail, winglets, engine cowls and cheatline. So that the stands and the ground equipment still judge what is drawn, each type's shape in `src/shared/airframe.ts` is now that model's, measured by `tools/models/airliner-shape.ts` (the fuselage's outline, the wing and tailplane as tapered surfaces bounding every point across every triangle of them, flaps, winglets and fillets included, the engines, the gear and the doors) and scaled the same way; `test/outline.test.ts` checks every triangle of each model as drawn lies within 2 cm of its outline, that no corner of the outline is more than 1.6 m from anything drawn, and that the livery slots paint the fin and not the cabin. The jet bridges and the stairs now go to the drawn front door (Polyfork's is 6.8 m behind a narrow-body's nose, where the old rule put it 4.5 m back), and the equipment was moved where the truer shapes asked: a narrow-body's aft belt loader ahead of the catering truck, a wide-body's fuel truck outboard of its dolly trains, the bus clear of an E175's wing.
- 2026-11-26: **Ground equipment on Polyfork's vehicles** — the block-built equipment round parked aircraft is replaced by fifteen of Polyfork's airport vehicles: a power unit at the nose; at a narrow-body a belt loader and a baggage tug with three carts of bags, at a wide-body container loaders at both holds with dollies of containers; a catering truck at the rear door before boarding, a lavatory truck at the tail early in the stay; a hydrant dispenser outboard of the inner engine (a tanker at remote stands); at stands without a bridge, stairs at the doors (pressed down to a narrow-body's door height) and an apron bus while passengers come off and go out; and a tow-bar tug, or for a wide-body a towbarless tug cradling the nose wheel, for pushback. Each type's whole layout is worked out once from the drawn aircraft and each piece shows while its task goes on; `test/gse.test.ts` keeps every piece clear of every other and of the wings, engines, tailplane and fuselage for every type, and holds each piece's box to its converted model.
- 2026-11-26: **Review 1's second batch of repairs** — after a change of visibility an arrival is no longer let in behind a super-heavy sooner than its wake allows (landing slots and the gaps behind them are held as stretches of time in the beat they were given in); the stands are judged by each type's own shape as the model draws it, set out once in `src/shared/airframe.ts` for the model, the stand rule and the benchmark; the benchmark counts an arrival in the flare as landing; the fuel truck under a low wing stands ahead of it, clear of the baggage carts; and the controls check watches the clock run at the start and the departures after a runway closure. Each has a test that fails with its defect put back; the day-long benchmark passes on eleven seeds ([review 1](../work/0_sfo-simulation/reviews/1_integration.md)).
- 2026-11-26: **People move by the characters' own animation, and the late run for their gates** — the model converter now reads Polyfork's dataset index in the 3d-maker checkout (credit, page and licence from each entry, any model not free refused) and takes every character's built-in Walk, Idle and Run clips (16, 12 and 12 frames) in place of poses made here; the stride each clip covers is measured from how far a planted foot moves back, and a walker's or runner's frames are timed to the ground they cover, so feet stay planted; standing people play the Idle clip, each at their own point in it. In the simulation, of travellers with no bags to check, 12 in 100 come only 20 to 45 minutes before departure, and anyone not at the gate in the last 12 minutes before the doors close runs for it at 3.2 to 4 m/s, the Run clip's own pace (with children, a brisk walk): in the crowd test's 75 minutes of a busy morning nine do, and nobody misses a flight.
- 2026-11-26: **Bridges with cabs, more people, and the close-ups made lighter** — a jet bridge's tunnel now stops a cab's length off the fuselage and its cab turns square to the door, so a bridge coming from ahead of an aircraft no longer runs into its nose (the final sweep's worst defect); a station's roof clears the highest track under it; nine more of Polyfork's characters (commuters, students, a business visitor, a casual traveller, a barista and a shopkeeper) join the crowds; and close up only the people round what the camera looks at are drawn as characters, the furniture in tiles that are culled out of view, so a close-up draws 2 to 5 million triangles where it drew 47 million.
- 2026-11-26: **The terminals furnished and peopled, with Polyfork's models** — the owner found the interiors bare and the lines unnaturally neat ([defect register](../learning/defect-register.md)). The terminals now have check-in desks with their bag belts and the airlines' walls, kiosks, security lanes with x-ray belts and scanners, passport booths, gate desks, 14,000 seats at the gates, carousels in the claim halls, shelves and tills in the shops, counters and tables in the cafés, armchairs in the lounges, plants, bins, trolleys and stanchions; glass round the buildings and walled shop fronts. They are Polyfork's free low-poly models (CC BY 4.0, credited in `public/models/CREDITS.md`), and so are the people near the camera: travellers, children, agents, security officers, police, crew and ground staff, walking, standing or sitting and each dressed from a table of outfits. People sit while they wait at their gates and board by groups (the longest boarding line went from 75 parties to about 15 on a narrow-body), browse shelves, eat at tables, wait round their flight's carousel and pull their suitcases; in a line each stands their own way and steps up when a gap opens. `test/interiors.test.ts` covers the class.
- 2026-11-26: **The repairs reviewed again, and what that found** — a second round read every repair of the first ([review 1](../work/0_sfo-simulation/reviews/1_integration.md)). Repaired from it: Caltrain trains no longer brush past each other on tracks side by side (shared track is now found by the cars' true size, as the test now measures them); people who ride BART off the map get off it; a driver who misses the turn for the curb goes round for it, and every planned stop is counted as made or missed; the roads day counts the traffic that gets through, not just what came in; a partial rebuild of the map data is judged by the sections it leaves out. Still being repaired: wake spacing behind a super-heavy across a change of visibility, the stand rule's wing shapes, the benchmark's runway check during the flare, a fuel truck parked through baggage carts, and two control checks that could not fail.
- 2026-11-26: **What the picture and the controls got from the review** — fog, low cloud and storms now show in the sky and the light; a click picks whatever aircraft is under the pointer, wingtip to tail; jet bridges swing to the forward door of the aircraft actually parked; trucks keep clear of the wings; the International Terminal's glass and the garage by the AirTrain no longer flicker; BART's viaduct comes down to its tunnel instead of ending in the air; the arrivals cut-away shows its floor from any distance; the camera stays over mapped ground; H keeps the map credit; F only follows. `npm run visual` now measures the frame rate with the clock running at 08:00 and fails below the plan's limits (59.9 fps at 1× and 60× in every view measured), and `tools/visual/controls.mjs` checks 30 things through the real controls, among them the camera, a click on a wing and the cut-away levels.
- 2026-11-26: **Trains, roads and people after the review** — passengers who come or go by AirTrain or BART now ride the trains; trains take shared track one at a time and turn round at the end of a spur without folding through themselves, and the Blue Line runs the other way round the terminals, as the junction to the car parks allows. Cars changing lanes no longer pass through each other, a green light no longer waits for a red one, drivers who miss their curb go round again, and a day of roads judges itself: nobody stands over 206 s, both freeways carry their demand. Late passengers no longer skip security, security has the lanes its busiest hour needs, staff hand over their posts at each shift, and the shops close at night. A day of roads and a day of people now run in `npm test`.
- 2026-11-26: **Independent review of the whole airport, and the airside after it** — both review lanes read the repository at b0b42a4 (Codex in five lenses, Claude with five subagents) and a subagent looked at all 72 captures: 112 findings, recorded with their dispositions in [review 0](../work/0_sfo-simulation/reviews/0_integration.md). On the airfield: a departure is never released towards an arrival coming the other way after a change of wind (one did, on the benchmark's storm scenario); big aircraft wait rather than take a runway too short; stands keep parked aircraft a metre apart at their true size, wings and all. The benchmark now judges runway conflicts every step by the tower's own rule, aircraft touching at their true size and time lined up; all 11 seeds pass. A closed runway still takes the aircraft already sent to it, by design.
- 2026-11-26: **An operations panel, flight boards and an aircraft inspector on screen** — the Operations panel (O) shows the runway plan and changeover, wind and cloud, landings and take-offs an hour, delays, aircraft waiting, gates in use, every checkpoint's lanes, line and wait, bags in the system and on the belts, road speeds and an event log, and carries the controls: four kinds of weather, each runway open or closed, each checkpoint open or closed (a closing one works through its line first, and a refusal says why). Flights (B) shows departure and arrival boards; a row, or a click on an aircraft, opens its card, and F follows it with the camera. What the viewer sets is kept across a jump in time. `tools/visual/controls.mjs` drives all of it with real mouse and keyboard on the GPU: 18 of 18 checks pass. Aircraft of foreign airlines carry their home countries' registrations.
- 2026-11-26: **Weather and closures change what the airport does** — a southeast wind turns the airport to the Southeast Plan (landing 19L and 19R, taking off from 10L and 10R) after a 15-minute changeover, with flights already on their way finishing the old way; low cloud or fog leaves one arrival stream and the delays show on the arrivals board, which now gives a time to flights still waiting for a landing slot; a runway can be closed to new traffic; and a security checkpoint can be closed, its travellers sent to the nearest open one, now that the concourses are joined after security as SFO's are (A to D and E to G; the map has no building between D and E). Wide-body departures get their gap in the landings sooner, by moving an arrival not yet on its approach back one place, which brought the longest wait on the 11 benchmark seeds down to 12.5 minutes. `tools/sim/scenario.ts` tells the story of any such change hour by hour. The controls are not on screen yet.
- 2026-11-26: **Aircraft keep apart on the ground and keep moving** — the hard-problem search ended with ground cut into tiles that fuselages hold, one order of right of way over all journeys, runways treated as strips of ground, and each landing's way to its stand fixed before touchdown. After it: stands are chosen against the ways in and out an aircraft would really take; traffic may cross a departures-only runway in front of a lined-up departure; departures line up in the order they reached the hold lines; landing slots are given out only a few minutes ahead, so a departure needing a gap in the landings gets one soon; and the schedule fits its departures to the runways, at most 44 an hour. The benchmark now also fails a departure held at its stand over 20 minutes. All 11 seeds pass: no fuselages meet, the longest wait is 15 minutes, the longest gate hold 15, and go-arounds are at most 4 a day. [Plan](../work/0_sfo-simulation/plan.md).
- 2026-11-26: **Passengers, bags and staff go through the terminals** — departing travellers in parties walk three floors from curbs, garages and stations through check-in, security and the shops to their gates and aircraft; arrivals go from the aircraft through customs and baggage claim to the curb; staff stand at desks, lanes, booths and gates while they are open, and shop staff and cleaners work by shift. Over a full day on seed 3, 99,858 departing and 98,707 arriving people are all accounted for and none misses a flight, the 95th-percentile waits are 10 minutes at security, 14 at check-in and 24 at customs, and 0.45% of bags are mishandled. Key I cuts the roofs away to show the floors. Road errands are not yet tied to passengers.
- 2026-11-26: **Roads and trains run, and the airport has night traffic** — about 1,700 vehicles at the morning peak on US-101, I-380 and the terminal roads, at 53 to 62 mph on the freeways with nothing standing over three minutes; 1,100 drop-offs and 1,000 pick-ups at the curbs in two hours. Northbound US-101 had no way off the map until ends of the network were told by where a link leads. AirTrain runs both lines, BART and Caltrain run; trains on a loop had been driving past their stops. Cars, trucks and aircraft carry their lights at night. Errands are not yet tied to passengers.
- 2026-11-26: **Aircraft fly the day but do not yet keep apart on the ground** — 589 visits a day land, park, turn round and leave from 91 usable gates; the new benchmark `tools/sim/soak.ts` fails on all five seeds: fuselages meet dozens of times a day, and two seeds still lock up. More than five repairs did not converge, so a hard-problem search is open in the [plan](../work/0_sfo-simulation/plan.md), one branch continuing.

- 2026-11-25: **The airport stands in 3D, by day and by night** — runways with their markings, taxiways, 104 gates with jet bridges, terminals, garages, the elevated roads and the AirTrain guideway are built from the layout into about 460,000 triangles and draw at 60 fps on the GPU at 1920×1080. The sun follows the clock; at night the airfield lights, apron floodlights and street lamps come up. Nothing moves yet. [Plan](../work/0_sfo-simulation/plan.md).
- 2026-11-25: **The real SFO is in the repo as six layout files** — built from OpenStreetMap: 4 runways within 1% of their published lengths, 1,207 taxi nodes, 221 stands, 324 mapped rooms inside the terminals, 2,842 road edges with 115 signals, AirTrain, BART and Caltrain. Two things the map gets wrong were fixed in the build, not by hand: 18 pairs of taxi nodes a few centimetres apart, and 5 buildings drawn twice.
