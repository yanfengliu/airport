Defects reported by the owner, each with the symptom as they saw it, what the investigation found, and the check that covers the defect's class from now on. Unlike a lesson, an entry stays after it becomes a gate: this is the standing list of what the gates could not see, which is where the next defect comes from.

## Bare terminals and lines too neat to be natural (2026-11-26)

**Reported symptom:** "Airport needs seats, conveyor belt, luggage, stores, etc. I am not seeing those. Also why are people lining up so neatly? This is not natural." And then: "The interior is so basic and ugly right now." The owner pointed at Polyfork's low-poly models (polyfork.dev) for what objects and characters, and what art style, the airport should have.

**Investigation and cause:** The cut-away drew each level's floor with the mapped rooms as pastel patches, a small box at each check-in desk, a thin box and post at each security lane, and an oval belt in each claim hall; there were no seats, no shop fittings, no belts at check-in, no luggage with anyone, and people were two boxes. In the simulation, every queue put each party exactly on its centreline 0.85 m behind the one in front, the whole line stepped up together, and the crowd past the ropes stood on a grid. People waiting at their gate stood at random points, and the moment boarding opened every one of them joined the line: at gate B20 that was 75 parties in a 60 m strip. Nothing checked that the furniture an activity needs is there, or that a crowd is not regular, so none of this failed a test: the tests counted people and timed waits, and the captures were judged for broken geometry, not for what a terminal has in it.

**Correction and check:** The furniture is now simulation data (`src/sim/terminal/fittings.ts`), laid out by rule in the mapped rooms and at the queues: check-in desks with their bag belts and the airline's wall, kiosks, security lanes with x-ray belts and body scanners or arches, passport booths, gate desks, banks of seats at every gate, carousels in the claim halls, shelves and tills in the shops, counters and tables with chairs in the cafés, armchairs in the lounges, plants, bins, trolleys and stanchions, all at the sizes of the Polyfork models they are drawn with (CC BY 4.0, converted by `tools/models/polyfork.ts`, credited in `public/models/CREDITS.md`). Walkers keep out of furnished floor. People sit in seats, board by group, browse aisles, sit at café tables, wait round their flight's carousel and pull their suitcases until check-in and after claim; in a line each keeps their own spacing and stands a little to one side, steps up only when a gap opens, and faces the front; past the ropes the line becomes a loose crowd. Near the camera people are drawn as Polyfork's characters, posed walking, standing or sitting and dressed from tables of outfits. `test/interiors.test.ts` covers the class: every check-in, security, passport and gate server has its desk, lane, booth or gate desk; nearly every gate has its own seats and every gate has seats close; every claim hall has carousels with room round them; nine in ten walled shops, cafés and lounges are fitted out; nothing stands off the floor or inside anything else, and no seat is inside furniture; the models are the sizes the layout uses; no line keeps one spacing, one centreline or one facing; a line's overflow is not a grid; people waiting at their gates sit, in real seats, one party to a seat; boarding lines stay short; and luggage is carried before check-in and after claim, never airside. Whether it looks right is judged, as before, by the captures.
